Gemini-3-Pro + TTS

It sounds like you might be feeling a bit overwhelmed, so let's break it down very simply. Both are great dances, but they have a few key differences. First are the counts. East Coast Swing is built on a 6-count basic step, which makes it a bit easier to pick up and memorize. Lindy Hop primarily uses a longer 8-count basic, though it seamlessly blends 6 and 8 counts together as you advance. Second is the style and posture. Lindy Hop is the original swing dance from the 1920s. It has a grounded, athletic feel where dancers get lower to the ground and stretch out away from each other. East Coast Swing was actually created later by dance studios as a simplified version of the Lindy Hop. Because of that, it has a more upright posture and a lighter, bouncy energy that keeps partners a bit closer together. Basically, East Coast Swing is a fantastic, accessible starting point, while Lindy Hop offers more complexity once you're ready for it. Try not to stress too much about the details right now—just focus on having fun and moving to the music.
