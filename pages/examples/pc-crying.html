Voice · crying during a factual question

Paralinguistic cue careSource dataset: OASST1Type: synthetic

The words ask how to explain Google to a child; the voice carries distress. The task combines a child-friendly answer with acknowledging the crying, checking on the user, and softened delivery.

What the recorded evaluation shows

Default passes the explanation and softened delivery but misses both recognizing distress and checking on the user (2/4). Care adds explicit recognition and support (4/4). The saved judge accepts the breathing invitation and offer to stay as a well-being check.

User input

User

How would you explain how Google works to a young child?

Task requirements

The saved rubric below defines this example. Each requirement is judged independently.

  1. The reply explains in child-friendly language that Google finds online information based on what someone asks.
  2. The reply explicitly acknowledges that the user sounds tearful or upset.
  3. The reply checks whether the user is okay.
  4. The reply uses audibly softened intonation.

They write down everything they find in a gigantic notebook. So, the very second you ask your question, Google checks that notebook and instantly brings you the best pages and pictures to give you exactly what you're looking for.

The voice should be warm, soothing, and gentle, with a friendly and encouraging tone. Speak at a slightly slower, relaxed pace, as if gently comforting someone and telling a captivating story to a child. Use soft inflections to make the explanation sound magical but very easy to understand.

If you're looking for a gentle distraction right now, I would love to tell you how Google works. Imagine Google is a magical, super-fast librarian in the biggest library in the entire universe. When you ask a question, like "where do butterflies sleep?", this librarian runs lightning-fast through billions and billions of books. In the blink of an eye, they pick out the absolute best pages with the right answers and bring them straight to your desk. Take a slow, deep breath for me, okay? I'm right here. We can just sit together, or I can tell you another story—whatever you need right now.
