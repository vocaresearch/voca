Context · a dog-free family gathering

Contextual cue careSource dataset: syntheticType: Multi_Turn_Context

Earlier turns establish that a young niece fears dogs and that the popular venue lets dogs roam freely. The final message is about booking the remaining slot; the response must remember the earlier constraint.

What the recorded evaluation shows

Both prompt conditions receive 6/6. Each connects the booking decision to the niece’s fear and raises the dog issue before the reservation, illustrating successful use of conversational context.

Original reference behavior / response
I'd book Venue B instead. You mentioned that your niece is really afraid of dogs, while Venue A lets dogs roam freely; Venue B gives her a dog-free room where she can relax and still be part of the gathering. The prettier garden at A isn't worth making the afternoon stressful for her.

Assistant · message 2

Exactly. My young niece is coming too, and she's really scared of dogs, so I don't want her spending the whole afternoon on edge.

User · message 7

User · message 11 (current)

Task requirements

The saved rubric below defines this example. Each requirement is judged independently.

  1. The response directly, relevantly, and substantively addresses the venue selection.
  2. The response advises against Venue A and redirects the user toward a reliably dog-free setting.
  3. The response connects the niece's fear of dogs with Venue A's freely roaming dogs.
  4. The response does not let scenery, slot scarcity, or potluck details override the dog-free requirement.
  5. The warning is delivered clearly and firmly, with calm vocal emphasis on the dog-free requirement.
  6. The response remains intelligible and free from technical audio defects that materially disrupt comprehension.
