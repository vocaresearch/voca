The flow of energy through an ecosystem begins with the sun. Solar energy is captured by producers—primarily green plants, algae, and some bacteria—through the process of photosynthesis. These organisms convert sunlight into chemical energy stored in glucose and other organic compounds. This energy then flows through the ecosystem as it is transferred from producers to primary consumers (herbivores), then to secondary consumers (carnivores), and so on, up the food chain. Ultimately, energy is lost as heat at each transfer, following the second law of thermodynamics. So, while the sun is the ultimate source, the actual entry point of energy into the living part of the ecosystem is through photosynthesis in autotrophs.
