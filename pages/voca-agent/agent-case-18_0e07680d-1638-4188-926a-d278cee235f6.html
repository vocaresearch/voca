I'm really sorry you're carrying this weight. It's completely normal to feel regret after an argument with your son—especially when he's 18 and navigating such a pivotal time in his life. That age brings independence, identity formation, and emotional intensity, and it can be hard to balance love with firm boundaries. Here’s what might help: **1. Acknowledge your feelings without judgment.** It’s okay to feel bad. That means you care. Self-compassion is the first step toward healing. **2. Did I feel unheard or overwhelmed?* Understanding the root—without blaming yourself—can help you grow. **3. Reach out with humility, not defensiveness.** A simple, sincere message like: *"Hey, I’ve been thinking about our conversation the other day. I’m sorry if I came across as harsh or dismissive. I care about you deeply, and I want to understand your perspective. Can we talk when you’re ready?"* This opens the door without pressure. **4. Listen more than you speak.** When you do talk, focus on understanding his experience. Use phrases like: *"I hear that you felt [emotion]. Can you help me understand why?"* This builds trust and shows you value his voice. **5. Reaffirm your love and respect.** Even if you disagree, remind him: *"I may not always agree with you, but I always love you. You’re my son, and I’m here for you—no matter what."* **6. Give space if needed.** If he’s not ready to talk, honor that. Respect his boundaries. Sometimes, silence speaks louder than words. **7. Consider a family therapist.** If arguments keep happening or emotions run high, a neutral third party can help you both communicate more effectively. Remember: You’re not failing as a parent. You’re human. And the fact that you’re reflecting, feeling, and wanting to make things right? That’s love in action. You’ve got this. And you’re not alone.
